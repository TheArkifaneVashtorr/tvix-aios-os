# Concept 2026-09-22a — `rules-validate`'s word-overlap check can pass a pin that has actually drifted

**Class:** validator gap, deferred. **Status:** proposed (found while judging the aios-public-build plan, not yet speced).

**Origin (Ship phase, 2026-09-22):** Judging `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-22-aios-public-build.md`, all three judges independently re-ran the draft's cited command, `nix develop -c python3 pkgs/evidence/rules.py validate docs/ledger/rules.toml --root . --verbatim`, and found it printed nine `source line does not contain the rule text` lines on `HEAD fd9df99b`, not the eleven the draft claimed three times (Assumption 9, D13, OS3 Facts). The other two of the eleven brief-pinned rows — `R-invariant-reproducible` (pinned `docs/brief.md:74`) and `R-invariant-publish-gate` (pinned `docs/brief.md:80`) — are just as stale as the other nine (their real text sits 43 lines later, like every other row after that morning's §2 rewrite), but the validator does not flag them: `rules.py`'s `_word_in` check accepts a pinned line whenever it shares even one word with the rule's text, and by coincidence the drifted line at 74 still contains "flake" and the one at 80 still contains "public" — words the rule text also uses, about an unrelated sentence.

**Idea:** `_word_in`'s any-shared-word matching is a correctness gap, not just an imprecision: a rule's `source` pin can rot arbitrarily far from its rule text and the check stays green for as long as the wrong line happens to share one common word (in prose about invariants, brief conventions and gates, common words are exactly what's likely to recur by chance). Tighten the match — e.g. require a higher fraction of the rule's distinctive words, or a contiguous phrase match, or track a content hash of the pinned line at authoring time and flag any change to it at all (a stricter, non-`--verbatim` default) — so a pin silently surviving a document rewrite is the exception the check exists to catch, not a coin flip on shared vocabulary.

**Payoff:** without this, every future `docs/brief.md` edit that shifts line numbers (like the §2 rewrite that produced this plan's ten flagged rows) can leave a handful of rules pinned to the wrong sentence with `rules-validate` reporting green — silently defeating the ledger's own reproducibility guarantee for exactly the rows unlucky (or lucky) enough to share a word with their real text. The nine/eleven discrepancy found here cost three independent judges a re-run each to catch; a stricter check would have caught it in the same `rules-validate` run this plan's step 0(a) already performs.

**Dependencies:** `pkgs/evidence/rules.py` (`_word_in`, `validate --verbatim`), `docs/ledger/rules.toml`'s `source = "docs/brief.md:<n>"` pins — no new ledger keys needed if the fix is a hash rather than a stricter word rule.

**Earliest landing:** a small Knowledge/Evidence task after this plan's step 0(a) lands (which re-pins all eleven rows correctly regardless of which the current check flags) — not urgent, since the false-negative only hides drift on rows that happen to share a word with their stale line, and the eleven pins are correct again once step 0(a)'s +43 shift lands.
