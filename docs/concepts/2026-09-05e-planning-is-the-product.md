# Concept 2026-09-05e — planning is the product

**Class:** process. **Status:** designed, spec pending the operator's review (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-planning-agent-design.md`, revised the same night after two critiques; no task typed yet). **Origin:** the operator, 2026-09-05 late: "planning is so important we should do research and then make another Fable agent to plan out exactly what a good planning agent does. The key factor in this dark factory is intelligent design but automatically. It's the 4-star service of software: predicting what the user will want or what the user would do. This can be integrated in dsh and Claude Cowork with a pinned workflow. The core value of the project is efficiency through information, planning and rigor." — and the day's record: 132 gate reviews, 59 rejected, 42 of them traced to the plan (vacuous 14, missing case 14, underspecified 8, wrong fact 6).

**Idea.** Seats and gates are metered and replaceable; the plan is the one artefact every downstream cost multiplies, so it is the product and gets a gate of its own. A plan is written from a packet, not from memory: the derived graph (`check`, `brief`, `waves`, `conflicts`), the evidence bundle, the claims, the seat's brief extractor, the record of past rejections by class, the field — the board's incidents since the last plan and the seat driver's own contract, read in full — the operator's stated preferences and pauses, and the spec; deterministic inputs first, model judgement last, as the operator ordered for scheduling. Each section is written for a reader who sees only Global Constraints, Assumptions and that section (which is exactly what `factory-brief` gives a seat), with one mutant per assertion, a discriminating row per fixture, an error contract per interface, and a rule where the last plan had a list of spellings. The plan then anticipates: the dispatch line and the landing recipe (branch reset to the one named commit, main merged in when the base moved), the switch's closure delta as a prediction, the operator's own steps as one command each, the gate's mutation table, the seat's known deaths and the prepared relaunch, the hooks and deny rules a task adds and what they will refuse, the claims it can close, the questions the operator will be asked — each with the acceptance that shows it worked and the date it becomes measurable. Where the driver can refuse a known failure deterministically (an unset `FACTORY_PLAN`, a board commit on a task branch, a misspelt result block), the plan names the guard task, not a sentence told to a model. Before dispatch a blind panel (two Sonnet, one Opus) scores fourteen rubric rows; below 34 of 42, or below 2 on the six rows that account for the plan-caused rejections, or with a judge silent after one re-ask, the plan is revised, never dispatched. A re-plan under rule A1 runs in a replan mode that rewrites the plan whole with every prior typed heading intact. The same process is a pinned Cowork workflow and a dsh skill with a profile; both are judged by the same panel; the score is filed and joined to the tasks' gate outcomes so the rubric learns which rows predict landings.

**Payoff.** Today 48 % of chains land first try and 19 % need a re-plan; one task took five gates and four hours. Every avoided rejection saves a seat run, a gate of 64k–182k Opus tokens and 40–65 minutes of wall clock, and the operator stops meeting failures live. Measured targets: first-try ≥ 70 %, plan-caused share of rejections < 40 %, re-plans < 10 %, zero mutants surviving outside the plan's named set, at most one unprepared operator command per plan (counted from the operator's own messages).

**Dependencies.** The derived task graph (G-series, landed); the orchestrator guard (OG1r2b, live; its rule is that no typed heading disappears); the driver guards (P11) before the first plan is dispatched through the agent; the JavaScript formatter and linter (P12, closing the open claim) before the workflow's `lint` acceptance means anything; the telemetry store's `gates` front matter and policy check (T1/T3), its `tasks`, `incidents` and `activity-days` streams (T2/T7/T4) for the anticipation rows' acceptances, and T10a/T10b for the report — none yet planned; `factory-brief` unchanged; the Workflow tool's named scripts under `.claude/workflows/`; the dsh-harness gate for the skill and the re-scoping of its `writing-plans`; a `plan` role in `route.py` and the six harness files in lockstep — no routing row until the measurement plan (3 specs × 2 samples × 2 models) exists.

**Earliest landing.** The draft linter (`tasks.py check --draft`), the operator-model file, the driver guards and the JavaScript lint are independent of each other and of the telemetry plan; they can run as one wave beside the seat-behind-broker work. The packet script and the record ledger follow the linter; the pinned workflow follows those and waits for B1 and SB4 to leave `flake.nix`. Its first plan is Helm Home sub-project 1, as decided on the board, typed as its own plan when the operator answers the spec's three questions.
